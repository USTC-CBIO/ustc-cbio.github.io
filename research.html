CBIO LAB · RESEARCH

Artificial intelligence for spatial omics.

We develop generative and representation-learning methods that expand what spatial omics can measure, reconstruct, and reveal.

Our approach

Biological omics is rich but incomplete: measurements can be sparse, tissue is often observed only in two dimensions, and high-throughput assays remain expensive. We combine biological priors, generative learning, and multimodal representation learning to recover missing information while preserving cell identity and tissue context.

01Measurement enhancement

Recovering informative gene expression

Subcellular spatial assays capture cellular architecture but measure only sparse gene panels. We use large single-cell atlases as transferable priors to reconstruct genome-wide expression, denoise observations, and improve cell-state discovery without requiring a matched reference for every tissue.

02Continuous reconstruction

Rebuilding tissue in true three dimensions

Physical sectioning destroys the continuity between adjacent slices. We model tissue evolution as a continuous vector field, align transcriptomic and proteomic sections, and generate cellular states at arbitrary depth to recover spatial domains and microenvironments in three dimensions.

03Cross-modal inference

Reading molecular states from histology

Routine H&E slides are abundant, while spatial transcriptomics is costly. We develop efficient generative models that learn co-expression structure and infer spatially resolved gene distributions from morphology at both spot and single-cell resolution.

04Intelligent omics

General models for biological omics

We extend these ideas toward multimodal biological foundation models that connect single-cell profiles, spatial measurements, pathology, and prior knowledge. The emphasis is on models that generalize across tissues, platforms, resolutions, and biological questions.

Current focusMultimodal learning

How we work

Three principles guide model development and evaluation across our projects.

Biological grounding

Start from a meaningful biological limitation and validate predictions with independent molecular or spatial evidence.

Generalization

Test across tissues, disease contexts, platforms, and resolutions instead of optimizing for a single benchmark.

Open and reproducible

Release code, models, documentation, and evaluation resources so results can be examined and extended.
